## D4 Derivation And Canalization

An independent D4 derivation pass is a natural bounded follow-up, with the same
input-isolation, negative-control, sealing, and downstream-comparison discipline
required by the GOV-517 decision. D4 target contacts and all D5 successful
outputs must not leak into generation. Register the D4 semantics independently;
do not simply substitute a tier label and presume success.

**A D4 pass does not distinguish canalization from derivation.** Both accounts
could be compatible with the same unperturbed geometry. Neither GOV-517 nor the
existing D4/D5 chain audit licenses a canalization prediction. Here canalization
is only a proposed robustness question, not an established mechanism.

| Design element | Definition required before a discriminating test |
|---|---|
| Competing accounts | Operational models that produce different outcomes under at least one independently motivated, admissible intervention |
| Intervention | Exactly what input or generative operation changes, what stays fixed, and why the altered object remains within the declared test domain |
| Readout | Registered geometry/signature unit, distance or equality, and robustness criterion; do not confuse unchanged office coverage with unchanged contact identity |
| Predictions | Each model's outcome or distribution derived before comparison, with provenance not fitted to D4/D5 results |
| Controls | Untouched baseline, invalid-input rejection, and controls separating invariance built into the quotient from actual response to perturbation |
| Interpretation | Equal model predictions mean nondiscriminating design; disagreement in predictions plus valid execution permits only the registered bounded comparison |

No operational competing models or discriminating intervention are supplied by
the reviewed receipts. Thus canalization discrimination is also blocked at
definition stage. A D4 pass could extend bounded derivability evidence; a D4
failure could bound that registered route. Neither outcome alone establishes
or rejects canalization, universal derivation, or a unified operator.
